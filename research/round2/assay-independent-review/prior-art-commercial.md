# Independent commercial and novelty evidence — decision-aware segmentation review

Reviewer: independent Astra agent; 2026-09-18. This memo was written before viewing the candidate benchmark's results. It does not award a pursuit pass or adjust the frozen protocol. A formal review requires frozen results, code/provenance inspection, exact component rights, and reproducibility evidence.

## Provisional conclusion

There is a narrow plausible practical wedge: given an existing segmentation pipeline, assay-specific decision thresholds, and a limited review budget, rank images by estimated downstream decisions corrected rather than segmentation uncertainty alone. This is a useful-combination hypothesis, not a claim of unprecedented science. Existing tools already connect images, segmentation, QC, phenotype features, and human review. The novelty claim survives only if a credible same-input comparison establishes a meaningful specific advantage and a buyer can use it without rebuilding their assay. A successful BBBC proxy benchmark would justify considering a small research pilot; it would not establish actual biological decision improvement, staff time savings, willingness to pay, or a durable moat.

## Paid category and economics

[MIT's high-content imaging fee schedule](https://ki-sbc.mit.edu/high-content-imaging/pricing), effective 1 September 2025, charges assay development/data analysis at $138.60/hour for KI and $184.80/hour for other MIT users. This establishes allocated spending on skilled analysis, not a price obtainable by this entrant. It also lists initial methodology/feasibility consultation as free.

[BIDMC's fee schedule](https://research.bidmc.org/immunostaining-and-microscopy-core/fees) charges image-analysis workstation service work at $61/hour internally, $91.50 external academic, and $122 external corporate; independent workstation use costs only $5, $7.50, and $10/hour respectively. The distinction matters: the candidate would need to save skilled service labor or decision rework, rather than merely substitute workstation access.

Genedata is evidence of a commercial software category, but the reviewed product pages provide no public dollar price. Do not manufacture a subscription benchmark. Pricing an initial scoped retrospective audit around $500–$1,000 is a testable proposal, not market evidence. A $750 audit would need roughly six hours of external corporate service-equivalent value at BIDMC's posted rate, before considering procurement and implementation overhead. An equal-image-count simulated budget does not establish those hours.

## Accessible validation population and distribution

Identifiable research imaging/screening cores include MIT Swanson high-content imaging and BIDMC Immunostaining and Microscopy Core. They are primary-source examples of a buyer population with paid analysis workflows, not enrolled design partners, sales leads with verified interest, or evidence of access to private datasets. Community distribution could use an opt-in CellProfiler/napari research tutorial or image.sc discussion if later authorized; this task performs no outreach. Public BBBC data makes the technical validation population accessible immediately, but that must not be conflated with access to customer operators.

The likely buying role is the image-analysis/core director responsible for analyst capacity, or an assay development lead at a small screening CRO. Budget ownership, existing review burden, export availability, data rights and procurement speed remain pilot questions. A broad enterprise platform replacement would be harder to sell and validate than a retrospective queue/report on an existing exported assay.

## What would justify a bounded test

Only after the frozen benchmark passes its stated advantage gate and independent code review: consider a 14-day, at-most-32-engineer-hour retrospective pilot, with at most $900 incremental cash. Explicitly value labor separately (e.g. $3,200 at $100/hour); do not call the fully loaded experiment less than $1,000. Three operators from two independent research facilities would be a useful target, contingent on consent and permission to use de-identified research images and assay definitions. If fewer than two facilities and one rights-cleared dataset are available by day 5, stop rather than invent an accessible population.

Before touching their outcomes, freeze actual assay decisions, held-out batches, a strong current manual/uncertainty comparator, and equal measured review time. Measure decision errors after analyst correction and analyst minutes, not just segmentation overlap. Require at least 20% fewer residual wrong assay decisions at the same review minutes with no increase in the agreed serious-miss category, or at least 25% fewer minutes for equivalent residual error; preserve per-facility results. Require at least one buyer to agree to a paid follow-on scope at a specified price. These are proposed future criteria, not permission to modify the current benchmark and not observed evidence.

## Defensibility concerns

The ranking method is short and incumbent-reproducible. A defensible business would need rights-cleared assay calibration cases, integrations, correction provenance and trust from recurring use. None is yet established. Practical combination novelty can still be meaningful without scientific novelty, but it must be demonstrated by the benchmark and then by an operator workflow; the clinical paper and established commercial stack lower the credibility of a broad differentiation claim.

## Formal judgment remains pending

The frozen protocol is well scoped in stating that nuclear count/median-area thresholds are research-QC proxies, perfect correction and equal review cost are assumptions, and ensemble perturbations are sensitivity draws rather than calibrated probabilities. Its fixed-budget same-input baselines and no-retuning rule allow a useful falsification. These precautions do not predetermine a result. A failed advantage gate means no pursuit pass for this implementation. A passed gate requires evaluating the remaining v2 floors independently, without promoting unmeasured labor or commercial hypotheses into facts.
