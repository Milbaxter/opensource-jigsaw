# Buyer and commercial source screen

Observed 18 September 2026. Public source inspection only; no sign-up, upload, purchase, contact, or product execution. No profitability claim.

## Buyer task and price pressure

An [Upwork buyer posting](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Quick-Task-Compress-Optimize-Heavy-GLB-Models-for-Shopify-Remodeling_~022091785499043108499/) dated 24 August 2026 offers $50 for a batch of three roughly 70MB e-bike GLBs, with color variants, to fit below 15MB for Shopify. Requested work includes texture resizing, possible WebP conversion, limited simplification of hidden parts, and Draco export while retaining visible geometry. This is an advertised one-time budget, not proof of completed payment, recurring demand, or demand for KTX specifically. It supports a concrete purchased deliverable and a potential recruitment channel, while arguing against assuming high per-model pricing.

A [2023 practitioner discussion](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/incredible-compression-on-a-glb/56996) describes inheriting a client project and struggling to reproduce its compact GLBs. Replies explain existing channel-aware recipes and tradeoffs. It supports historical workflow complexity and strong practical alternatives; it supplies neither measured labor nor present customer demand for our proposed mechanism.

## Closely related offers and existing capabilities

[SpatialPack](https://www.spatialpack.dev/) advertises $39/month Pro, rendered multi-view quality checks, and cohort recipe tuning. Its catalog service advertises $15/model after three free models, with a ten-model minimum, or $12/model at 25 or more. These are vendor offers. Its public claims have not been independently benchmarked here. Astra found accessible package source describing automatic quality-gated search; exact capabilities and licensing need to be incorporated into any prospective comparator. No code from its restricted core is included in Jigsaw or treated as an open-source product dependency.

[GLBStudio](https://glb.studio/) advertises $29/month for per-texture KTX2 controls, custom recipes, revision history, and API access; its site describes a visual comparison with quality metrics. This establishes a nearby offered price and feature competition, not independently verified execution or paid adoption. Several documentation links resolve to placeholders, so the marketing page alone cannot substantiate more detailed algorithmic claims.

[Advertflair](https://tools.advertflair.com/gltf-optimizer/) offers a browser optimizer and a separate catalog-scale service covering texture/geometry compression and GLB/USDZ delivery. No service price is given on the inspected page. This identifies an agency workflow and potential competitor or buyer role, without establishing that the agency wants our component.

## Implication for the proposed test

The legitimate question is whether a reproducible local integration offers a measured advantage at a useful output constraint and acceptable total cost. Rendered comparison, recipes, and automatic tuning already exist. Merely adding those features, beating an untuned preset, or quoting a broad $42,000 game-optimization suite would not establish a meaningful narrow business advantage. The eventual study must include the strongest accessible automatic alternative, preserve material behavior, and keep digital fidelity measurements separate from customer acceptance or labor savings.
