# Temporary roadworks planning: source-only HOLD

2026-09-18. Scope: up to three cross-field mechanisms for a human-reviewed construction-stage traffic assessment. No SUMO run, calibration, trained model, downloaded executable, deployment, performance comparison, or third-party contact occurred. Only primary-source reading, repository metadata/source inspection, five public rows, and aggregate coverage queries were performed. No protocol is proposed yet and no pursuit score is assigned.

**Decision: hold this lane.** There is authentic practitioner pain and an existing paid workflow. The obstacle is a precise practical benefit that can be tested with accessible evidence against competent existing alternatives. Known algorithms or suppliers do not disqualify an integration opportunity; an uncalibrated demonstration would not establish one.

## Buyer and workflow evidence

An [Australian temporary traffic designer](https://www.reddit.com/r/civilengineering/comments/1klankc/traffic_flow_analysis_how_to_measure_bottleneck/) describes six-month roadworks, a continuous lane closure and reduced speeds, and needs approximate hourly delay/queue estimates to discuss with stakeholders. The practitioner lacks paid tools and can use SUMO. A reply recommends FREEVAL. This is a concrete need for screening under limited resources, not evidence of willingness to purchase this proposed product.

[Taper](https://taper.co.nz/traffic-management-plan-software-vs-consultant) sells a reviewed TMP drafting workflow and distinguishes complex jobs needing consultants. [Civiease](https://civiease.co.nz/resources/tmp-cost-nz/) lists indicative design/submission ranges from NZ$250–600 for generic plans to NZ$1,500–3,500+ for complex/state-highway work. These are supplier category evidence in New Zealand, not an Australian transaction, traffic-model-specific price, achievable price, or candidate ROI. The buyer hypothesis is a small traffic-design consultancy that repeatedly prepares complex construction-stage assessments. No named prospect or acquisition channel is validated.

## Direct prior art and competent alternatives

[Hilbrich et al. 2025](https://elib.dlr.de/215933/1/2647_Hilbrich_et_al.pdf), DOI 10.52825/scp.v6i.2647, already connects construction records in Baustellenatlas to SESAM/SUMO, edits lane/speed/closure properties and compares scenarios. Its Berlin case uses daily-count calibration, but lacks systematic independent travel-time/route-choice validation. Reported user feedback identifies a real gap: local route repair poorly represents adaptation to long-term works; allowing wider rerouting loses the calibrated route pattern. This is stronger evidence than an imagined interface deficiency. It does not supply a simple validated remedy. The article is CC BY 3.0 DE; no public release of the calibrated scenario was verified. The initially supplied sumo.dlr.de/2025 URL returned a missing page; the DLR PDF above is the verified replacement.

[sumoITScontrol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23240) already supplies conventional traffic controllers, tuning and repeated-run analysis. Its [source](https://github.com/DerKevinRiehl/sumoITScontrol) is pinned in `baseline-source-pins.json`, GPL-3.0 as reported by GitHub and preserved LICENSE. A new uncertainty mechanism must beat good replicated parameter search/common random numbers and relevant fixed/actuated control, not an untuned single run. Temporary lane closures need not require any new controller.

[PennDOT's FREEVAL-PA workflow](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/penndot/about-penndot/advisory-committees-boards-and-commissions/state-transportation-innovation-council/innovations/predictive-work-zone-analysis) already addresses staging, diversion, closures and performance screening. [Its official FAQ](https://docs.penndot.pa.gov/Public/Bureaus/BOO/TSPortal/Questions.html) distinguishes quick planning from detailed calibrated analysis and limits free distribution/use to Pennsylvania/PennDOT contexts. It is not universally free OSS. A competent HCM/queueing screen still belongs in a comparison; microsimulation is not automatically the right tool.

[BO4Mob](https://github.com/UMN-Choi-Lab/BO4Mob) provides SUMO demand calibration with PeMS sensor measurements and SPSA/BO/SAASBO/TuRBO alternatives. It is useful adjacent benchmark infrastructure, not independent roadworks-plan outcome truth. The code and separate data repository LICENSE files say MIT; the [paper datasheet](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=YKQXu4JAYn) says CC BY 4.0 for the benchmark dataset and points to original PeMS/ETH terms. This discrepancy is recorded rather than resolved by treating a code license as a universal underlying-data grant. No benchmark payload was downloaded.

## A genuine public-data reserve, with limits

[FHWA dataset ag7u-9n3t](https://data.transportation.gov/resource/ag7u-9n3t.json), DOI **10.21949/1404187**, is publicly accessible. Its preserved metadata explicitly identifies public-domain licensing and Maryland highway coverage. Attribution: U.S. Department of Transportation, Federal Highway Administration (2024), *Data for Artificial Intelligence: Data-Centric AI for Transportation: Work Zone Use Case Processed Integrated Traffic Work Zone Data*, provided through ITS DataHub/Data.transportation.gov.

The aggregate query returned **2,845,040 rows**, **5,975 distinct workzone IDs**, timestamps **2016-01-03 through 2019-12-29**, **1,911,260 non-null volume values**, and **2,387 non-null max_lanes_closed values**. These are field-coverage counts, not validity rates. Other closure fields exist; sparse max_lanes_closed alone does not prove closure data unusable. Five sampled rows have speed/travel-time values and no volume. They occur before the associated closure, showing that rows cannot simply be treated as active-workzone outcomes.

The official [CC0 curation source](https://github.com/usdot-jpo-codehub/Data-Centric-AI-for-Transportation-Work-Zone/blob/766d64f084b01953198eb0b4cae987c485043daa/data_curation.py#L175) joins `MD_AADT_2019_adjust.csv` by TMC, weekday and time (lines 175–202). Accordingly **volume is not established as contemporaneous measured inflow**. Speed confidence fields also require interpretation before calling every value independent measurement. No directly observed queue-length field or alternative-plan outcome was found in the inspected schema. Observations support a possible prediction study; counterfactual plan quality, diversion, Australian transfer and six-month adaptation remain unsupported.

The [FHWA modeling guide](https://ops-dr.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop12009/sec4.htm) makes demand, capacity/discharge variability, network extent and field calibration central. Public input availability does not eliminate those requirements. A purported optimizer could optimize model error.

## Reopening condition

Reopen only with a specific reviewed deliverable and either a reusable calibrated public workzone case with independent observations, or a narrower prediction/calibration claim that the available data can actually test. The comparison must include the simplest competent workflow and measure its actual output quality or decision reliability, without converting source-code counts or invented analyst timings into customer savings. Until then, keep the data/source pins as a reserve and move to another domain.
