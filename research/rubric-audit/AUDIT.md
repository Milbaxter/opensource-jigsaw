# Independent audit of pursuit rubric v2

**Recommendation: retain the frozen thresholds and gates. No conceptual flaw sufficient to justify lowering the bar was established.** V2 is a selective authorization for a named business-validation experiment, not a ranking of every useful research action or a profitability forecast. Its wording accommodates practical integration value. Recent failures primarily expose experiment-design and scoring-calibration risks, not a requirement for new algorithms.

No candidate is rescored here. No previous result or prospective success criterion changes.

## What the rubric gets right

The decision is explicitly limited to a budgeted experiment. Payment-category evidence is separated from entrant demand; conversion and repeat use may remain testable uncertainties. Defensibility requires a concrete acquisition/test path, not an established moat. Rights and essential-input access must support the experiment, rather than the entire eventual business. Independent inspection, recorded failures and a strong alternative protect against impressive but useless demonstrations.

“Useful capability or measurable benefit” and “strongest practical alternative” already permit a known algorithm to produce a valuable integration. Faster preparation, fewer reconciliation errors, a usable import/report package, traceability or dependable operation on a neglected input type can qualify. Mathematical superiority is not required. The strongest baseline might itself use the same algorithm.

This is consistent with the user's interest in surprising OSS combinations: surprise must arise from a consequential connection, not the obscurity or number of repositories. Each dependency must materially enable the capability; every dependency need not independently outperform a competing library.

## Concrete error modes

| Risk | How the decision could go wrong | Correct interpretation / prospective protection |
|---|---|---|
| **False negative: algorithm-only benchmark** | A planner must find cheaper routes even when the proposed purchase is a reviewed bid-preparation package. Equal-quality output with less preparation might be valuable. | Before choosing a benchmark, identify the purchased deliverable and its claimed benefit. A workflow claim requires measured workflow evidence, not a route-cost proxy alone. A failed route-cost protocol stays failed. |
| **False positive: proxy becomes customer value** | A replayed synthetic counterexample or optimum is treated as shipping savings, useful defect incidence or analyst labor saved. | Trace the chain from technical output to buyer action to value. Mark each link observed, proxy-supported or untested. The commercial experiment must directly test the material untested links. |
| **False negative: an incumbent exists** | An existing supplier or known technique is treated as sufficient rejection despite a neglected integration, channel or delivery need. | Incumbents establish both demand and a strong alternative. Reject an unsupported benefit claim; do not infer that every practical niche is served. |
| **False positive: polished evidence substitutes for relevance** | Exact hashes, many controls and a successful bridge inflate feasibility/evidence while the customer-facing advantage rests on weak proxies. | Evidence scores concern support for decision-critical claims, not artifact volume. More synthetic cases cannot repair missing setup, access or demand evidence. |
| **False negative: future durable value must already be owned** | A permissioned corpus or repeatable service asset is rejected solely because it is unacquired, even though the experiment is meant to acquire/test it. | V2 asks for a concrete plausible source plus acquisition/test path. Missing ownership alone is not fatal; unspecified consent, reuse rights, acquisition cost or repeat value can make the path too speculative. Apply the same standard across candidates. |
| **False positive: any cheap informative trial qualifies** | A small budget and some possible learning are used to excuse an undifferentiated stack or inaccessible inputs. | Informativeness is insufficient. The named experiment must resolve a decision-critical uncertainty, have a realistic recruitment/input route, and plausibly change the next investment decision. Technical and commercial floors still apply. |

## What the representative reviews show

- **dbt, 76:** the review permits a conditional $3,150 fully loaded study despite withholding a pursuit pass. That is coherent if “watch” allows lower-priority information gathering. The hand-coded surrogate and absent real-model setup advantage are substantive limits. Requiring real workflow evidence does not require a superior fuzzing algorithm.
- **Cartonization, 76.5:** independently valid witnesses establish a useful narrow capability, not superiority of Hypothesis over random search or savings on present customer engines. Lack of a durable service advantage remains relevant. However, calibration should distinguish “no owned corpus yet” from “no concrete feasible corpus-acquisition/reuse path”; only the latter directly conflicts with the stated floor. This audit does not decide whether its proposed path met that floor.
- **MEP, hold:** zero wins against the frozen comparison and the independently reproduced obstacle-omission defect justify withholding that experiment's pass. They do not refute a future workflow-integration business. The current benchmark was intentionally narrower than the possible service value. A newly evidenced labor-saving proposition would need a new protocol, inputs and safeguards, not a revised interpretation of these results.

There is a calibration tension worth documenting: dbt's prospective consented profiles received a defensibility-floor score while cartonization's prospective regression corpus did not. Different access/reuse evidence could justify that distinction. The rule should require the judge to identify that difference explicitly, rather than silently switching between “plausibly acquirable” and “already acquired.” This is a consistency issue, not evidence that either numeric score should change.

## Prospective clarification, without threshold revision

For a future round, append a short **claim-to-test declaration before protocol selection**:

1. **Purchase and benefit:** buyer, deliverable, and one primary claimed improvement: output quality/cost, preparation/review burden, or another explicit capability. Name what the buyer currently uses.
2. **Fair comparison:** same underlying information, admitted constraints and required output quality; charge both methods for input preparation, corrections, operator work and support relevant to the claim. Identical byte formats are not necessary when format integration is the proposed benefit.
3. **Measurement:** identify the reproducible comparison that tests the claimed improvement. If operator labor is the benefit, actual operator/task evidence is needed; source-code length or author estimates do not establish it. A technical proxy supports only the stated technical claim.
4. **Remaining investment decision:** identify what uncertainty the proposed paid/consented study resolves, and what observed outcome would stop or advance work. List the actual route to any defensibility asset, permissions and acquisition cost.

This declaration clarifies existing gates 2, 4 and 7. It neither waives the executed bridge/advantage requirement nor supplies retroactive alternative success metrics. Record it prospectively before candidate evidence for the affected round, as the anti-shopping rule requires.

Finally, use three distinct decision labels: **continue discovery**, **run an explicitly limited research test**, and **validation-ready under the frozen pursuit gate**. The first two do not constitute a pass. A fixed high threshold can sensibly select rare pursuits while cheaper research on watch candidates remains rational. V2 should not be described as proving that every rejected idea is a bad experiment.
